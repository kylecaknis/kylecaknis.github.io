Why is my WordPress site so slow? 7 causes and fixes

If your website takes more than three seconds to load on a phone, a lot of people simply give up and go to a competitor. Google notices too: speed is part of how it decides who ranks.

The good news is that most slow WordPress sites are slow for the same handful of reasons. Here they are, roughly in order of how often I see them.

First, measure it

Before changing anything, get a baseline:

  1. Go to PageSpeed Insights
  2. Paste in your website address
  3. Look at the Mobile score (it's the one that matters most, and it's always lower than desktop)

Under 50 is slow. 50–89 is okay but could be better. 90+ is great. Screenshot it so you can compare after.

1. Huge images

This is the number one cause by a mile. A photo straight off a phone can be 4–5MB. Put ten of those on a homepage and you've got a page that's heavier than some apps.

Fix: resize images to the size they're actually shown (rarely more than 1600px wide), and compress them. A free plugin like Converter for Media or ShortPixel (free tier) can convert your existing images to the lighter WebP format automatically.

2. No caching

Without caching, WordPress builds every page from scratch for every visitor. With caching, it builds the page once and hands out a ready-made copy.

Fix: install one caching plugin, not three. If your host uses LiteSpeed servers, use LiteSpeed Cache; otherwise WP Super Cache or W3 Total Cache are solid free choices.

3. Too many plugins

It's not the number that matters so much as what they do. One badly built plugin can add more load time than twenty good ones. Sliders, social feeds, chat widgets and "all-in-one" page builder add-ons are common offenders.

Fix: go through your plugin list and delete anything you don't actively use. For the rest, ask whether each one earns its place.

4. A heavy theme or page builder

Some themes load fonts, animations and scripts for features you never use, on every page.

Fix: lightweight themes such as Astra, Kadence or GeneratePress are a big improvement. Switching theme is a bigger job, so do the other fixes first.

5. Cheap or overloaded hosting

If your host is slow to respond, nothing else can fully make up for it. In PageSpeed, a poor "server response time" (also called TTFB) is the giveaway.

Fix: a decent UK host with LiteSpeed or good caching often costs only a few pounds more a month. A free CDN like Cloudflare also helps visitors far from your server.

6. Render-blocking scripts and fonts

Some scripts and fonts must load before the page can appear at all. PageSpeed lists them under "Eliminate render-blocking resources".

Fix: most caching plugins can minify and defer CSS and JavaScript. Turn these options on one at a time and check your site after each one, because occasionally they break a slider or menu.

7. A cluttered database

Years of post revisions, spam comments and leftovers from deleted plugins slowly bloat the database.

Fix: the free WP-Optimize plugin can clean it up safely. Take a backup first.

Quick wins, in order: compress your images, add one caching plugin, delete unused plugins. Those three alone often move a mobile score from the 30s or 40s to 70+.

What about getting 100/100?

Be wary of anyone promising a perfect score. Real sites with real features (maps, booking widgets, analytics) rarely hit 100, and chasing it can mean ripping out things your business needs. What matters is that the site feels fast to your visitors and passes Google's Core Web Vitals.
