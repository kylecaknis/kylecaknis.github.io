Does a small business still need a website in 2026?

Plenty of small businesses get by with just a Facebook page or an Instagram account. So is a website still worth it? Here's an honest answer from someone who builds them, including when you might not need one yet.

What a website does that social media can't

People search Google, not Facebook. When someone types "café near me" or "plumber Stirling", Google shows websites and Google Business Profiles first. A Facebook page can rank, but rarely as well as a proper site.

You own it. Social platforms change their rules all the time. Posts reach fewer people every year unless you pay, and accounts get locked for no clear reason. Your website stays yours.

It answers the same questions all day. Opening hours, menu, prices, directions, "do you do gluten-free?". A good website answers these before anyone has to message you.

It makes you look established. Fairly or not, many people check for a website before they book, especially for trades, salons and anything that costs more than a coffee.

Tourists and newcomers find you. In places like Stirling with a lot of visitors, people planning a trip search Google Maps and websites, not local Facebook groups.

When you might not need one (yet)

Be honest with yourself. A website might not be your priority if:

  • You're fully booked through word of mouth and don't want more customers
  • You're testing a side hustle and haven't decided whether it'll last
  • Your customers genuinely all come from one place, like a market stall with regulars

Even then, a free Google Business Profile is worth setting up today. It's what shows on Google Maps.

What a small business website should include

You don't need dozens of pages. For most local businesses, one to five pages covers everything:

  1. Home: what you do, where you are and a clear button: Call, Book or Order
  2. Services or menu: with prices if you can, because people trust businesses that show them
  3. About: a friendly photo and a few lines. People like knowing who they're dealing with
  4. Contact: phone, a contact form that actually works, opening hours and a map
  5. Reviews: link to your genuine Google reviews

Most importantly, it has to work properly on a phone, because that's where most of your visitors are.

What does it really involve?

There are three parts:

  • A domain name (like yourbusiness.co.uk): usually around a tenner a year
  • Hosting, where the site lives: from a few pounds a month for a small site
  • The website itself: built once, then occasionally updated

I build on WordPress because it's free, it runs a huge share of all websites, and you can edit your own text and photos afterwards without paying someone every time. You also own everything: the domain and hosting are in your name, not mine.

The cost of not having one

It's hard to measure customers you never hear from. But if someone searches for what you do, can't find you, and finds your competitor's tidy website instead, that's a sale gone, and you'll never know it happened.

In short: if you want new customers to find you on Google, a simple, fast, mobile-friendly website is still one of the best investments a small business can make. It doesn't have to be big or expensive.
