Small business website checklist: 12 things to check

Most small business websites don't break all at once. They slowly drift: an old phone number here, a contact form that stopped sending there, a page that takes ages to load on a phone. Nobody tells you, and customers just quietly go elsewhere.

The good news is you can check the important things yourself in about 20 minutes, with free tools. Grab a cup of tea, open your website on your laptop and your phone, and work through this list.

1. Search for your business on Google

Type your business name and town into Google, the way a customer would. Does your website come up near the top? Is the short description under it accurate, or is it something odd like "Home – Just another WordPress site"?

If the wording is wrong, that's your page title and description. Most WordPress SEO plugins (Yoast or Rank Math, for example) let you change them in a couple of minutes.

2. Check your Google Business Profile links to the site

On the same search, look at the box on the right (or at the top on a phone). Make sure the website button goes to your current site, and that your opening hours and phone number match what's on the website. Different details in different places confuse customers and Google.

3. Test your speed on a phone

Go to pagespeed.web.dev, paste in your address and look at the mobile score. Under 50 usually means visitors are waiting too long and leaving before your page appears.

The most common causes are huge images, no caching and too many plugins. I've written a separate guide on why WordPress sites get slow if your score is low.

4. Open it on your own phone

Most visitors are on a phone, so this matters more than how it looks on your laptop. Check:

  • Can you read the text without zooming in?
  • Are the buttons big enough to tap easily?
  • Does anything spill off the side of the screen?
  • Does the menu open and close properly?

5. Send yourself a message through the contact form

This is the one that catches people out the most. Fill in your own contact form and press send. Did the email actually arrive? Check your spam folder too.

WordPress forms often stop sending quietly after a hosting change or an update, and you'd never know until someone mentions they "messaged weeks ago".

6. Tap your phone number

On your phone, tap the phone number on your website. It should start a call straight away. If it doesn't, the number isn't set up as a clickable link, and you're making customers copy it by hand.

7. Read your key details out loud

Go through your opening hours, prices, services, address and staff names. Are they all still true? Out-of-date details are one of the quickest ways to lose trust, especially if someone turns up when you're closed.

8. Look at the first screen only

Open your homepage and don't scroll. In those first few seconds, can a stranger tell:

  • What you do?
  • Where you are (if you're local)?
  • What to do next: call, book, buy or get a quote?

If the answer isn't obvious, a clearer headline and one main button often make more difference than a full redesign.

9. Click around for broken links and pages

Click every item in your menu and footer. Look out for "Page not found" errors, buttons that go nowhere, and links to old social media accounts you no longer use. A broken link on a key page makes a site feel abandoned.

10. Check Google can see all your pages

If you haven't already, set up Google Search Console. It's free. Under Pages it shows which of your pages Google has indexed and flags any it can't reach. If important pages are missing, customers can't find them through search.

11. Check the little things that look neglected

  • Is the copyright year in the footer still 2021?
  • Is the latest "news" post from three years ago?
  • Are there placeholder bits left over, like "Sample Page" or lorem ipsum text?

None of these break anything, but together they make a business look less active than it really is.

12. Check updates and backups in WordPress

Log in to your WordPress dashboard and go to Dashboard → Updates. A long list of waiting plugin and theme updates means the site hasn't been looked after in a while. Before you update anything, make sure you have a recent backup. A free plugin like UpdraftPlus can do that. Then update plugins one at a time, so if something goes wrong you know which one caused it. If an update ever leaves you with a blank page, my guide to the WordPress critical error walks you through getting it back.

Short version: search for yourself, test the speed and the phone view, send yourself a message, tap your number, and read your details out loud. Those six checks catch most of the problems that cost small businesses customers.

What to do with what you find

Write down anything that didn't pass, then put it in order: things that stop people contacting you (a broken form, a phone number that doesn't tap) come first, then speed and mobile problems, then the tidy-up jobs.
